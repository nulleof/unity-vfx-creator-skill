# Review render and integration

Inputs are the brief, completed prefab and its clean-project capture. For blind reconstruction, wait for the candidate to be frozen before opening the reference. Check that reference and candidate use the same camera, duration, resolution, sampling and seed, plus the same preview trajectory where applicable. Compare draft and final for new variants.

For a gameplay prefab, watch the capture at gameplay speed, then inspect frames just before, at and after the focal moment. Check that the focal layer dominates at the intended time and that the remaining layers support it. Judge the shape, contrast, scale and material from the brief's camera. For a loop, check its appearance, several steady-state samples and release. For a trail, check the tapered path. For a variable effect, compare three seeds using the same camera and timing. Confirm that differences are readable and the main shape, event time and gameplay bounds hold across plays.

When a gameplay scene is available, also review on a light surface and a dark surface, beside an actor or target, and during an overlap with other effects. Check that the event moment, direction and gameplay footprint remain readable. Record the first critical mismatch with its time or frame, then assess silhouette, rhythm and style as described in [shape and timing](../references/shape-and-timing.md).

Put the scale reference in view with the effect. Compare the projected width and height of the defining shape with the reference, then check the full reach of the halo, wake and particles. Inspect contact, brightest moment, widest silhouette and residue in their authored order. Check that the character, target or area stays readable at the times the player needs it. Check that curves carry changes in motion, scale and opacity and that a flipbook shows the intended changes to the outline or internal gaps. For a flipbook, inspect the opening and final cells, anchor stability, any neighboring-cell bleed, and the handoff to other layers. A one-shot should finish without returning to its opening pose. For a loop, watch several transitions between repeats.

Inspect `effect.json` alongside the prefab and use the [playback sequence](../references/visual-brief.md#playback-in-unity) to check start, stop and reuse in Play Mode when available. The capture uses stepped simulation, so inspect the runtime lifecycle in Play Mode before claiming it has been verified there. Cite frames or times that show both the focal moment and its variation.

Render against a dark background. Inspect motion at speed and onset, peak, and fade frames. Run `Compare` for side-by-side frames, absolute differences, foreground IoU, color error and onset/peak/end timing. Check the import receipt for dependencies and any missing shader or script. A clean-project render with no visible output is a failure.

Use the foreground-area plot and pixel bounds to check alignment and scale. For each beat, record the onset, peak, end and projected width and height on representative frames. Check for quiet intervals and late shapes that outlast the flash. For a loop, report visible-frame coverage and typical silhouette area after buildup, then inspect motion across several cycles. For a moving effect, check trajectory, width and wake persistence at matched times. Check for clipping before attributing a size difference to the recipe.

Use the report to verify silhouette, changes in speed, palette, layer relationships and dissipation. Give frame or time evidence for the largest mismatches. Initial similarity is a human decision. Record per-case numeric limits after approval for regression checks, and report pixel equality independently. Keep the blind result available if a later revision uses the reference directly.
