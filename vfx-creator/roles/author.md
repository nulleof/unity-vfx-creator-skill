# Author an effect

Inputs are the brief, installed catalog and a clean Unity URP project. Return the draft and final recipes, prefabs and render directories. In a blind run, use the brief, catalog and your own renders as visual evidence.

For a gameplay prefab, use the [visual brief](../references/visual-brief.md) for the appearance, capture settings and playback mode. Build drafts under a separate ID in a disposable URP project with matching rendering settings when available. Design each independently triggered or attached effect as its own prefab. Keep the defining silhouette distinct from accent particles, and plan the fade according to when the caller stops emission. A sustained aura that should fade within half a second needs short-lived overlapping layers, including its persistent rim.

Decide which moment and silhouette carry the action before searching the catalog. If the request omits them, infer them from the trigger, playback mode, and gameplay distance. Use `Find` with a partial [visual signature](../references/signatures.md) for the brief's visual traits. Adapt a whole recipe when its main silhouette, movement and phases fit. If only the accent or palette matches, borrow the useful layers and synthesize the dominant shape and timing.

For each layer, decide its onset, emitter duration, particle lifetime, location, motion and change in size and opacity. Use the intended camera to translate the brief's image-plane dimensions into world scale. A ground-aligned ring projects differently from a camera-facing billboard. For a moving effect, set the path, orientation and speed first. Match trail thickness and taper along that path, then tune lifetime to control how much of the path stays visible.

Place the effect beside its scale reference in the gameplay camera. Block the main silhouette at the intended size, then extend the halo, wake and particles according to the brief. Use a short flash to mark contact and give the main shape room to reach its widest moment. Use local simulation for particles that follow their attachment and world simulation when emitted particles should stay behind. A layer that must spawn at a separate gameplay event or transform can use its own prefab.

For changing silhouettes, multiple beats or prominent motion, sketch the key poses and build a grayscale block-in with the gameplay scale and phase timings. Give the defining mass, contact and residue distinct values. Watch it at speed and record contact, widest pose, last frame of the defining mass and end of the residue. For a trail, also sample the head position, width behind the head and time from stop to clear. Use the frames alongside the capture timeline to distinguish the defining shape from the full halo or wake. Pick one measured discrepancy to correct before finishing the surfaces and accents. See [shape and timing](../references/shape-and-timing.md) for exercises and editable examples.

Use these patterns where they fit the brief.

- **Flash or impact.** Give the contact frame a dense, short-lived focal shape, then let the secondary layers take over as it decays. An organic flash can pair a compact `glow` burst with a second burst of `Stretch` particles. A small sphere emitter fans the particles out. Range their size, speed and lifetime independently so the rays spread at different angles, widths and lengths. For a graphic impact, set the distinctive outline and open space before adding glow.
- **Repeat variation.** Choose a few bounded differences that stay visible at gameplay scale. Use `randomizeOnPlay` when those differences should change on each full restart. For a tumbling mesh, use separate 3D initial rotation and spin curves. Keep the contact point and event time fixed.
- **Distinct beats.** Give each beat its own phase. Size and time the layers around the opening, widest silhouette and late afterimage of that beat. Preview the last visible frame of the first beat, the interval between beats, and the start, peak and final fade of the next beat.
- **Changing silhouette.** Use an original flipbook when the outline needs to open, bend, break apart or change its internal gaps. Draw the appearance, widest readable shape, breakup and residue first. Add intermediate frames if a jump distracts at gameplay speed. Use Particle System curves for translation, scale and opacity, and give the flash, wake and debris their own layers when they need independent timing or placement. Follow the [texture sheet setup](../references/recipes.md#animated-texture-sheets).
- **Sustained effects.** Give the volume or rim a continuous anchor. Use rate-based loops for moving accents and overlap particle lifetimes with emission cycles so that movement continues across the loop. Use prewarm if the brief calls for immediate steady state. Check buildup, then inspect several late times for gaps, size drift and unwanted flashes.
- **Ambient volumes.** Use bounded emission and stagger the opacity and lifetime of the moving accents. For fire, separate the short bright base from embers and slower, expanding smoke. For drifting weather, vary size and speed across depth layers and check that the density settles.

Render the draft with the brief's framing, pitch, sampling and motion. Use the candidate's `capture.json` timeline to check foreground size, centroid, brightness and clipping at the brief's stated times. Inspect the rendered frames for shape orientation and major empty regions, then watch the action at speed. Correct silhouette scale, path and timing before tuning palette, density and small accents. If a larger billboard still renders at the same size, inspect its camera-relative `maxScreenSize`. If it touches the frame edge, check the camera and clipping before using the bounds as evidence. Rebuild and render the final with the same settings, and record any remaining deviations from the brief.

The editor bridge creates its own URP materials and masks. If a procedural mask misses an important shape, generate an original PNG from the recipe's prose, with transparent background and clear edges. Pass it using `-ResourceRoot` and record the relative PNG path in the texture recipe. A project receiving the prefab uses the copied texture in the prefab's output folder.

For externally triggered impacts or world-space trails, attach the corresponding prefab or named child to the gameplay event or moving transform as described by the brief. The preview rig can drive test motion and timing.
